Where I bring AI into a company

01

Choosing the use cases

I start from processes and people, not tools. I map the activities, estimate value and risk, and choose one or two cases to start from, with success criteria written down before we begin.

02

Prototyping in days

With Claude Design and Claude Code I build working prototypes for real users to try. Decisions are made on something you can touch, not on a slide.

03

Making AI trustworthy

Answers with sources, confidence thresholds, human review, automated tests on the outputs. The requirements of the EU AI Act, ISO 42001 and NIST AI RMF become concrete controls.

04

Driving adoption

Training on real work, champion colleagues, adoption KPIs collected without monitoring people. This is where many AI projects stall.

02

The method: six PMI-CPMAI phases

An AI project is a data project. That’s why I follow CPMAI, the Project Management Institute’s method: iterative, with a checkpoint at the end of each phase.

  1. IBusiness understandingWhich problem, for whom, and how we’ll measure success. If AI isn’t the right answer, we say so here.
  2. IIData understandingWhich data exist, where they are, who owns them and how reliable they are.
  3. IIIData preparationCleaning, permissions and privacy before any model.
  4. IVModel developmentA prototype built with AI agents, tried by users within days.
  5. VEvaluationAutomated tests on the outputs, edge cases, checking the criteria agreed at the start.
  6. VIOperationalisationRelease, training, adoption KPIs and monitoring over time.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

  1. What does an AI Product Manager do in an artificial intelligence project?

    They choose where AI creates value, get solutions built and validated, manage risk and compliance, and follow adoption. They don’t start from a tool: they start from the problem and the data.

  2. Where do you start when introducing artificial intelligence in a company?

    With an inventory: which AI tools people already use, often with personal accounts, and which repetitive tasks weigh the most. Then one or two small use cases, with a measurable success criterion and a prototype tested within a few weeks.

  3. Why do so many AI projects stop after the demo?

    Because technology is the easy part. What’s often missing is success criteria agreed in advance, reliable data, users’ trust and someone who follows adoption after the release. That’s why I measure real usage with dedicated KPIs.

  4. Does the AI Act require companies to train their employees?

    Yes. Since 2 February 2025, Article 4 of the AI Act requires providers and deployers of AI systems to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy among the staff who use them. It doesn’t prescribe a standard course: training must be adapted to the roles and the systems used. See how AI is learned at work.

  5. What is the PMI-CPMAI certification?

    It’s the Project Management Institute’s certification for managing artificial intelligence projects with an iterative six-phase method, from business understanding to operationalisation, with a focus on data and trustworthy AI.
